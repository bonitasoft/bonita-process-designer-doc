= Call other processes
:description: Point a Call Activity at another process, open the called process in a document tab, and keep references consistent when you delete a process or export to Bonita Studio.

{description}

NOTE: Called processes and document tabs are available from {productName} 1.1.

A *Call Activity* is an activity that runs another process. In {productName}, you point a Call Activity at another diagram of the application: the *called process*. You can then open the called process straight from the Call Activity, and the reference is kept when you export the diagram to Bonita Studio.

[IMPORTANT]
====
*Calling a process is not filing it.* Every process sits in exactly one place: at *Home* (the top level), in a folder, or under another process. That place is its *filing path* (see Organize processes in folders). A Call Activity does not move or file anything, and filing a process under another one does not create a Call Activity. The *Tree* view on the Home page shows how processes are filed, not which processes call which.
====

[#set-the-called-process]
== Set the called process

.Point a Call Activity at another process
. Open the calling diagram in the editor.
. Add a task from the palette, or select an existing activity.
. In the inspector on the right, open the *Content* tab and, under *Available shapes*, click *Call Activity*.
+
The element is replaced by a Call Activity.
. Click the Call Activity on the canvas.
+
The *Called process* section appears in the inspector, with the name of the activity.
. Optionally, type in *Filter processes…* to narrow the list by title.
. Select the process to call in the list.
+
The call is set as soon as you select it, and a notification confirms it: *This activity now calls "<process>"*.

The list offers every other process you have access to. The diagram you are editing is not listed. When there is nothing to offer, the section shows *No other process is available.*

The list shows titles only, and two processes can have the same title: they then look identical in the list. Rename one of them first, so that you pick the right one.

The called process is part of the diagram, like the position of a shape: it is saved with the diagram, it appears live for the people editing with you, and *Ctrl+Z* undoes it.

To remove the call, select the Call Activity and click *Clear* in the *Called process* section (*Called process cleared.*). To call a different process, select another one in the list. *Open* and *Clear* are shown only when the activity calls a process.

[#processes-that-cannot-be-called]
=== Processes that would create a loop

A process must never end up calling itself, directly or through other processes. A process that already leads back to the one you are editing is still listed, but it is greyed out and cannot be selected. It reads *<process> — already leads back here*, and a hint under the list explains:

[quote]
Greyed-out processes already lead back to this one; calling them would create a loop.

The check is also made on the server, when the change arrives. If someone else closed the loop a moment before you, your change is refused with *That process already leads back to this one, so the call was not applied.* A diagram whose calls lead back to itself cannot be saved either: the editor shows *Not saved: a process call in this diagram leads back to the diagram itself. Clear or retarget that call, then save again.*

[#open-a-called-process]
== Open a called process

A Call Activity that calls a process shows a small round button with a downward arrow on the canvas. Click it to open the called process. Hovering over it shows *Open the process called by "<activity>"*, or *Open the called process* when the activity has no label. The button is available to everyone who can open the diagram, Readers included.

Creators and Admins can also click *Open* in the *Called process* section of the inspector.

Before opening, {productName} checks that the called process can still be reached. If it cannot, you stay where you are and a message tells you why:

* *The called process no longer exists. It may have been deleted.*
* *You do not have access to the called process.*
* *The called process could not be opened. Check your connection and try again.*

[#document-tabs]
== Work with document tabs

A called process opens in a *document tab* inside the application, not in a new browser tab. As soon as a second process is open, a tab strip appears above the editor.

image::document-tabs.png["The top of the editor with two document tabs, Expense Claim and Expense Approval, the path Finance / Expense Claim / Expense Approval of the active tab, and the toolbar",800]

* The first tab is the process you started from. It has no close button.
* Click a tab to switch to it. A tab you leave keeps its state: zoom, scroll position, selection, undo history and unsaved changes are all still there when you come back.
* Opening a process that is already open switches to its tab instead of opening a second one.
* Opening a called process from inside a tab adds a tab to the same strip.
* A dot on a tab means that its process has unsaved changes.
* With the keyboard, use the arrow keys, *Home* and *End* to move between tabs, and *Delete* or *Backspace* to close the focused tab (except the first one).

You can have up to eight processes open at once, the first one included. At the limit, opening another one is refused with *You can have 8 diagrams open at once. Close a tab to open another one.*

To close a tab, click its *×*. If the tab has unsaved changes, the *Close this tab?* dialog asks what to do:

* *Save & Close* saves the tab, then closes it. If the save fails, the tab stays open with its changes.
* *Close Without Saving* closes the tab and discards its unsaved changes.
* *Cancel* keeps the tab open.

Leaving the editor, for example with *Home*, checks every open tab: if any of them has unsaved changes, the leave confirmation opens, and *Save & Exit* saves all of them before leaving.

Open tabs are not kept when you reload the page: the page reopens only the process shown in the address bar.

[#process-path]
== The process path in the editor header

The editor header shows the filing path of the open process: the folders and the processes it is filed under, from the top level down to the process itself. In a document tab, each tab shows the path of its own process under the tab strip.

* A process in the path can be clicked to open it. From a document tab, it opens in a tab. From the first tab, it replaces the page and closes the other tabs; if any of them has unsaved changes, the *Leave this diagram?* confirmation first offers *Save & Continue*, *Continue Without Saving* and *Cancel*.
* A folder in the path is plain text.
* A level you do not have access to is shown as *Process not accessible*.

The path tells you where the process is filed. It does not show the chain of Call Activities you followed to open it.

[#delete-a-called-process]
== Delete a process that other processes call

When you delete a process that other processes call, the delete confirmation lists them before you confirm: *1 process calls this diagram and will be left with a broken call:* (or *N processes call this diagram…*), followed by their names. Callers you do not have access to are counted but not named: *Including N processes you do not have access to.*

It may have been deleted.*, and the *Called process* section shows *Process not accessible*. Select another process or click *Clear*.

[#called-processes-in-bonita-studio]
== Called processes in Bonita Studio

When you export to BPMN, each Call Activity keeps its call: Bonita Studio finds the called process by its name. Give the processes you call distinct titles. {productName} warns you about some duplicate names, but not all. See xref:user-guide:export-and-import.adoc[Export and import diagrams].
