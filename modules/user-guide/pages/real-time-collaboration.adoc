= Collaborate in real time
:description: Edit the same diagram with your teammates at the same time, see who else is on the canvas, and let {productName} keep everyone in sync automatically.

{description}

In {productName}, several people can open and edit the same diagram at once. There is no need to wait for someone to "release" a diagram before you can work on it: everyone's changes are merged live and saved for you, and you can see who else is editing alongside you.

== Who can collaborate

Collaboration follows the same rules as everything else in {productName}: you can only join a diagram that belongs to one of your groups, and what you can do depends on your role.

* *Creators* and *Admins* can edit the diagram in real time.
* *Readers* can open the same diagram and follow along — they see other participants and their changes live — but cannot make edits.

For more on roles and group scoping, see xref:administration:permissions-and-roles.adoc[Roles and permissions].

== See who is editing

When other people are on the same diagram, {productName} shows their presence directly on the canvas:

* A *participant list* shows everyone currently viewing or editing the diagram.
* Each participant has their own colour, so you can tell contributions apart.
* You see other participants' *live cursors* moving across the canvas, and the element each person currently has selected.

This makes it easy to coordinate verbally ("I'll take the approval branch, you take the rejection path") while you work in parallel.

[#how-changes-are-kept-in-sync]
== How changes are kept in sync

The server holds the single source of truth for the diagram while you collaborate. As you add, move, resize, connect, or delete elements, your changes are sent to the server and broadcast to the other participants almost instantly. The server stores the diagram a few seconds after the last change, so you do not need to save to share your work.

When two people change *the same element* at nearly the same moment, {productName} resolves the conflict with a simple *last-writer-wins* rule: the most recent change to that element is the one that is kept. Changes to *different* elements never conflict — they simply merge.

If you edit very fast, some of your changes may not be shared, and the editor tells you: *You are editing too fast — some changes were not shared. Please slow down.*

[TIP]
====
Because conflicts are resolved per element, the safest way to work in parallel is to edit different parts of the diagram. The advisory lock indicator (below) helps you see when someone is already working on an element.
====

[#save-while-collaborating]
== Save while collaborating

*Save* (or *Ctrl+S*) and autosave still have a role while you collaborate, but they do not send your copy of the whole diagram, which could overwrite a co-editor's work. They send the diagram's preview image for the Home page, and the server then stores the shared diagram immediately rather than a few seconds later. Each time the server stores the diagram, a version can be recorded: see xref:user-guide:diagram-versioning.adoc[Version your diagrams].

A save is reported as successful only when the server answers it — which, after a network interruption, can happen even though some of your edits never reached it: see <<known-issue-dropped-connection>>. For the save button, autosave and their messages, see xref:user-guide:editing-diagrams.adoc#save-and-autosave[Save your work].

[#advisory-lock-indicator]
== The advisory lock indicator

When another participant is actively editing a specific element, {productName} can show an *advisory lock* on that element, with a label such as *<name> is editing this element*. This is a courtesy signal, not a hard block: it tells you "someone is working here right now" so you can avoid stepping on each other's changes. It does not prevent you from editing — collaboration stays fully concurrent.

[#ai-editing-lock]
== While AI is editing the diagram

When someone generates or modifies the diagram with AI, the AI result replaces the diagram: *Generate* replaces the whole diagram, and *Modify* works from what is on the diagram when the request starts. So that the result is not mixed with other edits, the diagram is locked for everyone else until the AI request is over:

* The other participants see a banner: *AI is editing this diagram for <name> — editing is paused until it finishes.* While the lock is on, they cannot change the diagram.
* Their palette, inspector, element tools, *Save*, *Undo*, *Redo*, *Open file* and *Simulate process* are paused. They cannot move shapes, edit labels, drop a file on the canvas or use the editing shortcuts (*Delete*, *Backspace*, *Ctrl+Z*, *Ctrl+Y*). They can still look at the diagram, export it and print it.
* A change that reaches the server during the lock is refused with *AI is editing this diagram — your change was not applied. Please wait.*

Wait until the banner disappears, then check the diagram before you carry on: what you see is the AI result.

The person who started the AI request is not locked. Analyzing a diagram with AI does not lock it. See xref:user-guide:ai-generation.adoc[AI diagram generation].

The lock lifts on its own when the request is over, whether it succeeded or failed — at the latest about a minute later if the person who started it has closed the editor in the meantime. If the AI service does not answer, the lock lasts until the request times out: about ten minutes by default. Nobody can cancel a running request. The banner names the person who started it: if the lock lasts, ask them first.

[#connection-problems]
== When the connection drops

If your network drops briefly, {productName} reconnects automatically. On reconnect, your editor receives the current diagram from the server and your canvas is replaced by it, so you immediately see any changes other people made while you were away.

While you are disconnected, you can keep working, but your changes are not shared with the other participants. Autosave and *Save* then store your copy of the whole diagram directly, but not in the shared diagram that the server keeps while anyone has the diagram open. As long as someone has the diagram open — a co-editor, a Reader, a second tab of yours, or a stale connection of your own — that shared diagram wins: when you reconnect, your canvas is replaced by it, and the changes you made while disconnected are lost.

[IMPORTANT]
====
Do not keep editing while you are disconnected. Reload the page and wait for the connection to come back. If you need to keep your changes, first export the diagram as *JSON* to keep a copy.
====

If the network is unavailable altogether, saves fail with *Failed to save diagram* and your changes stay flagged as unsaved.

After repeated failed attempts to reconnect, real-time collaboration stops with the message *Live collaboration stopped after repeated connection failures. Reload the page to resume.* If your session has expired, the message is *Live collaboration stopped: your session has expired. Log in again to resume.* In both cases, reload the page, logging in again if asked, to rejoin the diagram.

[#known-issue-dropped-connection]
=== Known issue: a dropped connection can look connected

The editor does not currently detect a connection that died without being closed, for example after your computer wakes from sleep or after a Wi-Fi drop. Such a connection still looks connected: the edits you make over it never reach the server, and *Save* can still report success.

After a network interruption, or when your computer wakes from sleep, reload the page before you continue editing.
